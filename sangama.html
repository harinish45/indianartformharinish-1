About the synthesis

The assignment asks for creative synthesis, so the artwork keeps Warli's composition — triangle bodies, the dance circle, red geru ground — and borrows Kalamkari's margins: indigo-madder vines, flaming lotus, fish borders, pen-drawn foliage. All paintings and captions here are original work made for this submission.

    From Warli

    The geometry

    • Bodies as two joined triangles; heads as circles; limbs as open strokes.
    • The tarpa dance circle as the engine of the composition.
    • White on red geru — one pigment, one ground, total contrast.
    • Sun, moon and palm standing as corner witnesses.
    The synthesis

    One surface, two mother tongues

    Sangama No. 1 keeps Warli's grammar — the circle dance, the triangle bodies, the red earth — and lets Kalamkari write its margins: indigo vines, flaming lotus, fish that swim the border. The tree borrows Kalamkari's leaf-by-leaf patience while the figures stay stubbornly Warli. Neither style illustrates the other; they share one surface the way two rivers share a delta.

    संगम — where the white line meets the indigo vine.

    From Kalamkari

    The pen

    • The bamboo-pen line: continuous, flowing, black.
    • Flaming lotus and scrolling vine as the border logic.
    • Indigo + madder dye palette on cream cotton.
    • The fish-and-lotus border band of Machilipatnam trade cloth.
